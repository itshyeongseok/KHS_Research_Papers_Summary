How Ornithopters Can Perch Autonomously on a Branch
Tier 0 — 핵심
Raphael Zufferey, Jesús Tormo-Barbero, Daniel Feliu-Talegón, Saeed Rafee Nekoo, José Ángel Acosta, Anibal Ollero
Nature Communications, Vol. 13, 7713, 2022 · DOI: 10.1038/s41467-022-35356-5
🔍 핵심 인사이트 보기
논문 요약
The paper reports the first autonomous branch-perching flight of a large flapping-wing robot. The P-Flap (700 g, 1.5 m wingspan, derived from the E-Flap) integrates a pitch-yaw-altitude flight controller, a TSL1401 line-scan optical close-range correction system, and a bistable carbon-fiber claw that closes on a 6 cm branch within 25 ms and applies 56.8 N. A 4-step development pipeline (launcher → flight-only → soft branch → real branch) yielded 6 successful perching flights out of 9 attempts at 2.5–3 m/s and 30° pitch, replicated with a second robot. Quantitative envelope: tolerates ±10–20° yaw deviation depending on leg pitch, branches between 4 and 11 cm diameter, impact forces below 150 N. The work establishes the canonical landing reference for the FWAV waypoint-mission terminal phase.

Introduction — 연구 동기 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Birds rely on perching to hunt, watch, rest and monitor — and similarly, a flapping-wing robot that could perch on a branch unlocks wildlife monitoring, microscopic branch-surface analysis, leaf sample return, energy recovery via solar, contact inspection of pipes and power lines, and persistent observation. Achieving this is hard because of unsteady aerodynamics, weight-strength scaling laws ($Lv^2 \sim 1$, $L^2$ vs $L^3$) and the lack of hovering at large scale. Prior work covers fixed-wing and multirotor perching but no large flapping-wing robot has perched on a branch from free flight before this paper.
Related Works — 관련 연구 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Perching on UAVs has been extensively reviewed; multirotor perching and fixed-wing wall perching are mature. Only the centimetre-scale Robobee has perched as a flapping-wing robot, and that under a flat ceiling using electrostatic adhesion. No large flapping-wing branch perching has been reported, and that is the gap closed here.
Method — 새로운 접근 방법 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The perching method composes three subsystems on the P-Flap (700 g, 1.5 m wingspan). (1) Bistable carbon-fiber claw with central spring that snaps from open ($\Psi_1$) to closed ($\Psi_2$) on contact, applying 56.8 N at the contact zone with a 50 mm rotation-point spacing $D_e$ and a 6 cm nominal branch diameter; spikes + Ecoflex soft pads provide friction. The claw closes in 25 ms; re-opening uses an integrated leadscrew motor (200 N tension, 20 s open). (2) Active leg driven by a 34 g servo with a diagonal spring shock absorber, optimised via PSO over a finite-element-validated dynamic model. (3) TSL1401 line-scan optical sensor on the claw, 1×128 px contrast detection at distance up to 190 cm, feeding a PD controller that steers the leg-claw at the elbow joint to compensate flight-altitude inaccuracy. Triple control loop at 120 Hz on the C++ autopilot for pitch Θ, yaw Ψ, height Z via elevator δ_e, rudder δ_r, and flap frequency Ξ_F.
Experiment — 실험 방법과 결과 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
A 4-step development pipeline (Fig. 4) — (1) launcher only, (2) flight-only without claw, (3) with soft branch, (4) full perching on real branch — provides safe, iterative system tuning. Launcher experiments characterise impact resistance up to 4 m/s with 100% perching success and impact forces below 150 N; close lock within 25 ms versus 50 ms total contact-to-bounce time. Yaw deviation tolerance ±10–20° depending on leg pitch; legs at horizontal (90°) widen the success envelope. Flight experiments demonstrate 6 of 9 perching attempts succeeded at 2.5–3 m/s on an 80 cm wooden branch at 2 m altitude, replicated with a second robot (without wing camber).
Discussion — 결과의 해석 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The first successful branch perching of an ornithopter is enabled by combining a controlled attitude/altitude flight controller, an actively-controlled vision-fed claw-leg, a bistable mechanical claw and a 4-step development pipeline. Outdoor cluttered environments and complex control architectures are the explicit next axes. The bistable mechanical design and the close-range correction trick are the most transferable single ideas.
Conclusion — 최종 요약 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Through advances in flapping-wing technology this work demonstrates the first successful branch perching flight of an ornithopter. Combined attitude/altitude control with fast tail action regulates flight at 2.5–3 m/s within 15 cm of the target; the actively-controlled claw-leg with vision feedback grasps within 25 ms with 2 Nm torque and a purely mechanical release. The recipe is the canonical reference for FWAV terminal-phase perching research, with outdoor migration, control-architecture upgrade and richer sensing as the explicit next steps.
