Introduction — 연구 동기 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Flapping-wing robots have a long history (Daedalus and Icarus, da Vinci, Borelli, Lilienthal, DeLaurier) but were overshadowed by fixed-wing and multirotor success and abandoned for decades. Research revived in the 1990s and 2000s with insect- and bird-inspired prototypes, expanding in the 2010s to insect-sized, small-scale and large-scale autonomous systems. The paper takes a robotics perspective, surveying not just design and mechatronics but the rising body of work on intelligent functionalities, and is positioned as complementary to prior reviews focused on narrower aspects (transmission, propulsion-power-control, hovering systems, hummingbird-class, deformable wings).
Related Works — 관련 연구 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The review treats prior FWFR reviews as complements, focusing each on a narrower slice (transmission, propulsion, hovering, etc.). It then synthesises the literature across those slices and adds intelligent functionalities. The 'related works' here is the broad FWFR literature rather than direct prior art on a specific method.
Method — 새로운 접근 방법 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The review presents the FWFR as an interconnected dynamical system: rigid main body + flexible wings + airflow + (in robots) control + onboard electronics + flapping mechanism. The unifying mathematical framework is on $SE(3)$ with the body twist $T = (\omega, v)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^6$ and dual wrench $W = (\tau, f)^\top$. The Euler-Poincaré equation governs main-body dynamics. A port-Hamiltonian framework unifies modeling, simulation, model-order reduction and model-based control across the rigid-wing-airflow chain. Aerodynamic modelling uses blade-element approximations or CFD; the limits of linear conventional airflow models from fixed-wing aviation are explicitly described. Energy-saving via flapping-gliding-soaring (including dynamic soaring exploiting wind shear) is treated as a separate design lever.
Experiment — 실험 방법과 결과 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The review surveys experimental work across three categories. Insect-sized: Robobee (Harvard, 100 mg, ~170 Hz, electrostatic adhesion perching), DEAnsect (dielectric elastomer), KubeetleS (tailless hovering). Small-scale: DelFly Nimble (Karasek et al., 28 g, torque-coupling banked turns), Robotic Hummingbird (Fei et al., onboard nonlinear control). Large-scale: E-Flap (510 g, 1.5 m, payload-capable), HIT-Phoenix (675 g, 2.3 m, formation flight), Robird (450 g, 18 m/s), RoboRaven (200–700 g, solar). Intelligent functionalities surveyed include visual servoing for line tracking and landing, SLAM, sense-and-avoid (Falanga, Sanket, Mueggler, Rodriguez-Gomez), autonomous navigation (bug algorithms, view familiarity, snapshot-based bio-inspired methods).
Discussion — 결과의 해석 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The review identifies weight as the universal restricting challenge, the inadequacy of fixed-wing-derived linear airflow models, the limited maturity of onboard intelligence, and the gap between simulation environments and reality (especially for RL). It points to event cameras as the perception of choice, port-Hamiltonian as the unifying mathematical treatment, and walking/jumping/self-righting legs as a way to relax landing precision constraints. Applications: monitoring, inspection, contact perching, last-mile delivery.
Conclusion — 최종 요약 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The review classifies FWFRs into insect-sized, small-scale and large-scale; presents dynamics, aerodynamics, control, intelligent functionalities, and applications; recommends port-Hamiltonian as the unifying framework and event cameras as the perception of choice; flags weight and onboard intelligence as the universal challenges; and points to monitoring, inspection, contact perching and last-mile delivery as the application portfolio that should drive the next decade of FWFR research.
