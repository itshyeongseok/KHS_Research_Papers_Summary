Introduction — 연구 동기 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Flapping-wing flight offers high maneuverability and agility but its complex unsteady dynamics make stable, efficient flight hard. The community has heavily studied modeling and control, yet trajectory generation for FWAVs remains underexplored relative to fixed-wing or rotary-wing aircraft, and the few existing planners are tied to particular platforms or scenarios — most importantly, they are not closed with a tracking controller. This paper sets out to provide both the planning theory and the tracking controller, and to demonstrate the first closed-loop 3-D real-flight integration on an underactuated FWAV.
Related Works — 관련 연구 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
FWAV trajectory generation papers are few and partial: closed-loop motion planning + dynamic inversion for perching (Paranjape et al.), bat-bot direct-collocation planning (Hoff et al.), two-stage fixed-wing-then-flapping optimization (Hoff and Kim), and graph-based energy-efficient ornithopter planning (Rodríguez et al.). None integrates with a tracking controller for real 3-D flight. Tracking work spans integral sliding mode (Wissa), neural networks (He), vector field path-following (Ndoye), and onboard nonlinear control (Fei) — each missing some combination of real-flight validation, underactuation handling, or time-coupled position satisfaction. Obstacle avoidance (Kong, Tijmons, Chen) exists in adjacent communities but is rarely composed with FWAV planning.
Method — 새로운 접근 방법 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Vertical-frame dynamics separate position–velocity (Eqs. 1–4) from heading dynamics (Eq. 5) and reveal a near-non-holonomic constraint $v_{Vy} \equiv 0$. The flat outputs $\sigma = (x,y,z,\psi)$ (or 3-D $(x,y,z)$) prove the FWAV is differentially flat. Trajectories are N-th-order polynomials per segment, optimised under minimum-snap with continuity (Eqs. 25–28), boundary, kinodynamic (Eqs. 31–32) and obstacle constraints solved by SQP. The tracking controller cascades a saturated PD-like outer loop ($v_d = \dot{\sigma}_r + K_p\tanh(e_p)$, $a_d$ in Eq. 44) feeding a reduced-attitude desired Γ, with a hysteretic $h_\psi$ and robust $\mathrm{sgn}(\omega_{\psi d}-\omega_\psi)$ heading law that ensures global practical asymptotic stability. Reduced-attitude normalisation preserves Γyd exactly without approximation.
Experiment — 실험 방법과 결과 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Validation uses a self-made 29 g X-wing FWAV with 34 cm wingspan, two linear-servo-driven elevator/rudder and a brushless flap motor. Trajectories are generated offline in MATLAB fmincon and tracked online from a ground station using a Qualisys arena (48 Arqus A12 cameras). Three flight cases — sphere obstacle, two cylinders, six waypoints — plus a uniform-speed straight line establish that heading-direction-change rate is the dominant residual error driver. Cases (a) and (b) track well; Case (c) suffers from rapid heading transients at waypoints 3 and 5; the straight-line case proves accurate tracking when heading is held fixed. Discussion — 결과의 해석 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The vertical-frame model and differential-flatness-based planner give a platform-agnostic recipe; the saturated cascade controller with hysteretic + robust heading law gives a stability-proven inner loop. The dominant residual error driver is heading-direction-change rate, exposing a planner–controller co-design opportunity. Validity hinges on $\Gamma_x > 0$, $|\Gamma_y|$ small (near-hover or near-pure-forward flight). Onboard migration and aerodynamic re-identification per platform are the citable open issues for the user's research.
Conclusion — 최종 요약 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The paper claims and demonstrates the first closed-loop integration of trajectory generation and tracking control for real 3-D flight of an underactuated FWAV at a practical level. Vertical-frame dynamics + differential flatness underpin the planner, a cascade Lyapunov controller closes the loop, and a 29 g X-wing platform validates obstacle avoidance, two-cylinder navigation and six-waypoint tracking. Future work targets onboard migration, perching, and incorporating heading-direction change-rate into the SQP objective.
