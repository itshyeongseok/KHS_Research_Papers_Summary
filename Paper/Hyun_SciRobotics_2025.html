Sticking the Landing: Insect-Inspired Strategies for Safely Landing Flapping-Wing Aerial Microrobots
Tier 0 — 핵심
Nak-seung P. Hyun, Christian M. Chan, Alyssa M. Hernandez, Robert J. Wood
Science Robotics, Vol. 10, eadq3059, 16 April 2025 · DOI: 10.1126/scirobotics.adq3059
🔍 핵심 인사이트 보기
논문 요약
The paper unites a crane-fly-inspired compliant leg design and an adaptive controller with τ-theory landing trajectories to enable safe, accurate landings of an insect-scale flapping-wing microrobot (Harvard RoboBee, 100 mg, 30 mm wingspan, ~170 Hz flapping). Hovering experiments quantify three ground-effect regions (strong > 7%, weak 4–7%, weakest < 4%) as a function of the dimensionless height ratio $H = h_{cop}/l_{wing}$, and the adaptive controller compensates the ground-effect-induced thrust shift. A two-joint Kapton-TPE compliant leg with 60% tarsal proportion is selected from 12 designs by minimising coefficient of restitution and lateral drift. Landing trajectories follow a $\gamma$-second-derivative profile $z(t) = (h_d/2)(1 - \tanh\alpha t)$, parameterised by acceleration rate $\alpha \in \{3,5,7,9\}$, and a leaf-to-leaf landing on a pothos plant demonstrates real-terrain operation. Limitations: uneven inclined surfaces, higher impact velocities and scalability to larger vehicles remain open.

Introduction — 연구 동기 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Insects efficiently transition between aerial and terrestrial environments via diverse landing strategies — from controlled deceleration in honeybees and fruit flies to crash landings in stingless bees and mosquitoes. Compliant legs play a key role in dissipating impact energy in real insects, and translating this to the insect-scale flapping-wing microrobot domain is the unsolved gap. The paper combines a crane-fly-inspired compliant leg design with an adaptive ground-effect-compensating controller and τ-theory-inspired landing trajectory to achieve the first systematic safe and accurate landing on natural terrain for the Harvard RoboBee.
Related Works — 관련 연구 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Ground-effect studies span fixed-wing aircraft, rotary-wing hovering, and 2D/3D simulations for flapping-wing flight; the boundary between in-/out-of-ground-effect was not previously quantified for insect-scale FWMAVs. Compliant landing gear is well-developed at kg scale (rotary-wing) but unexplored at insect scale. τ-theory landing trajectories are biologically established (honeybees, fruit flies, bumblebees) and have inspired engineering applications (UAV ship-deck landing, lunar landers), but ground-effect-aware τ-theory landings on insect-scale FWMAVs starting from rest were missing.
Method — 새로운 접근 방법 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Three threads: (1) ground-effect quantification via $H = h_{cop}/l_{wing}$ and $G = (1 - T_{avg}/T_{max}) \times 100\%$, with three regions (strong > 7%, weak 4–7%, weakest < 4%) determined from 27 hovering experiments. (2) Adaptive controller compensates model uncertainties and constant disturbances; demonstrated to improve lateral hovering accuracy over the baseline geometric controller from ref 19. (3) Compliant leg with Kapton-TPE damping joints (Kapton spring + TPE damper, sandwich layup) and 60% tarsal proportion, four legs in 30°/45°/60° stance, leg height 20 mm to keep $h_{cop} = 30$ mm in weak ground-effect region. (4) Landing trajectory $z(t) = (h_d/2)(1 - \tanh\alpha t)$ commands acceleration then deceleration via $\ddot\gamma$ sign change, with $\alpha \in \{3,5,7,9\}$ controlling aggressiveness.
Experiment — 실험 방법과 결과 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The experimental program splits into ground-effect characterisation (27 hovering experiments at $H \in (0.5, 3)$), leg-design selection via free-fall drop tests (12 designs × 6 trials each), landing-success classification (20 drop tests sweeping body roll), controlled landing flights ($\alpha \in \{3,5,7,9\}$ on smooth substrate), and a leaf-to-leaf real-terrain demonstration on a pothos plant. The Vicon T-series motion-capture supplies state estimation; a tether supplies power and control. CoR for the chosen leg 7 design remained similar across vertical landing experiments; impact velocities ranged from 0.04 to 1.9 m/s across the four α values; leaf-to-leaf landing succeeded with body orientation 6° at impact, within the success classifier.
Discussion — 결과의 해석 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The compliant-leg + adaptive-control approach pushes the boundary of safe and accurate landing for insect-scale FWMAVs and provides a directly transferable mechanical idea (passive energy dissipation) plus a directly transferable control idea (γ-second-derivative trajectory) for any flapping-wing platform that needs to land. Limits cluster around inclined surfaces, higher impact velocities, scalability and the need for onboard sensing.
Conclusion — 최종 요약 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The framework integrates passive multi-segment energy dissipation in the compliant leg with active adaptive control during landing on the RoboBee, demonstrated on natural terrain (a leaf). Future work spans inclined-substrate active-leg control, higher-impact-velocity tuning, scalability to larger vehicles and onboard sensing — all of which are open invitations the user can latch onto.
