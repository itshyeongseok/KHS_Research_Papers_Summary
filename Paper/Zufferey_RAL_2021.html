Introduction — 연구 동기 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Flapping-wing robots offer a quiet, soft and human-friendly alternative to propeller-based aerial vehicles, but flapping flight at the 1 kg class is hard: high torques on slow wings, turbulent unsteady aerodynamics and insufficient thrust without active or passive wing twisting. Existing small-scale ornithopters (Robobee, KuBeetle-S, BatBot) hover but cannot carry the autonomy stack; medium- and large-scale designs (Dove, Thunder I, RoboRaven, Robird, SmartBird) trade off payload, low-speed flight or robustness. The paper introduces the E-Flap to satisfy onboard reliable perception, 0.5 kg empty mass with 100% payload, ≤2 m/s minimum flight speed, 1.5 m wingspan and modular reconfiguration — none of which had been jointly achieved.
Related Works — 관련 연구 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The literature on flapping-wing robot platforms spans three scale brackets — small-scale insect-/gram-class hovering ornithopters (Robobee, KuBeetle-S, BatBot, Nano Hummingbird), medium-scale half-meter forward-flight platforms (Dove, Thunder I), and large-scale bird-of-prey-class platforms (RoboRaven I–IV, Robird Falcon, Robird Bald Eagle, Festo SmartBird). Each bracket has acknowledged contributions but none meets the joint requirements above; in particular, payload-capable medium platforms with ≤2 m/s flight do not exist before E-Flap. The aerodynamic and structural foundations come from Pennycuick and Hassanalian sizing methodology, classical wing-pitch–thrust theory (Garrick, Platzer, Smits), DeLaurier's efficient ornithopter wing, and Heathcote–Gursul's flexible flapping airfoil propulsion.
Method — 새로운 접근 방법 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The E-Flap structural design takes maximum payload as its primary criterion, bounded by 1.5 m wingspan and 1 kg total design mass. Aero-elastic double-elliptical wings (0.52 m² area, b²/S=5, 0.36 m root chord) use 6 mm CF spar at quarter chord and 1.5 mm CF chord rods at 15 cm spacing for passive 90° pitch phasing, with optional Dyneema-loop camber. The tail (0.12 m² horizontal) provides longitudinal trim plus elevator/rudder actuation; lateral roll is via dihedral. The composite CF-plate body wraps the transmission and tail; the wing root uses a Y-shaped wing-root + ball-bearing carbon central hinge for ~6.5 Nm peak torque. Powertrain sized via Hassanalian's f = ζm^{3/8}g^{1/2}b^{−23/24}S^{−1/3}ρ^{−3/8} (≈4 Hz target) and Strouhal-number stroke calculation (φ ≈ 32°) — a 1130 Kv brushless motor + 42:1 gear reducer + 4-bar linkage handles 130 W estimate / 200 W measured peak. Custom 10 g PCB integrates STM32 + NanoPi Air Neo + VectorNAV VN-200, with three regulated power buses and 95%-efficient TPSM53604 DC/DC.
Experiment — 실험 방법과 결과 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Validation spans mass + power distribution, payload-vs-trajectory tests with 50 g increments up to 520 g, max-performance flight envelope at 100% flapping frequency, cambered-vs-flat wing comparison at three throttle settings, lateral-directional maximum turning rate, and over 100 outdoor manual flights with GPS/IMU logging. Headline numbers: max steady $\gamma_{ss,max} = 40.18°$ at $\theta_{ss} = 58.92°$, max steady $V_{ss,max} = 5.63$ m/s at $\theta_{ss} = 9.80°$, max climb $\dot z_{ss,max} = 1.79$ m/s at $\theta_{ss} = 37.86°$, max turning rate $\dot\Psi_{max} = 2.08$ rad/s at $\gamma \approx 0$. Camber improves steady climb angle from 0° to 5.89° at 65% throttle and from 12.8° to 15.6° at 70% throttle.
Discussion — 결과의 해석 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The E-Flap is a payload-capable, low-speed, modular flapping-wing platform engineered specifically for autonomy + manipulation work. The CFD/Hassanalian sizing recipe is open and reproducible. The 65% gap between estimated and measured power highlights the limits of simplified aerodynamic modeling and the need to budget conservatively. Roll control via dihedral alone is the explicit lateral-directional bottleneck, motivating a dedicated roll axis in next iterations. Camber is a high-leverage design variable for slow-flight performance.
Conclusion — 최종 요약 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The paper concludes that the E-Flap is a major step toward a fully autonomous flapping-wing robot, validated by 5.5 Hz peak flapping, 70° peak pitch, 520 g payload, and 100+ indoor + outdoor flights. The design is engineered for cheap and quick standard-equipment manufacture, in two days from parts to assembly. Future work uses the modular platform for perching, obstacle avoidance, manipulation, human interaction and closed-loop outdoor flight with vision + GPS + IMU.
