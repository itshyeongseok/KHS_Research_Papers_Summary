FWAF-VID: A Flapping-Wing Aggressive Flight Benchmark Dataset for Visual-Inertial Localization
Tier 0 — Core
Jizhou Jiang, Erzhen Pan, Wenfu Xu, Wei Sun, Jingyang Ye
IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters, Vol. 10, No. 6, June 2025, pp. 6328–6335 · DOI: 10.1109/LRA.2025.3560856
Paper Summary
Jiang et al. introduce FWAF-VID, the first onboard visual-inertial benchmark dataset tailored for aggressive flight on a flapping-wing aerial vehicle. The dataset comprises 28 sequences (17 real-world flights, 6 calibration, 5 static flapping) collected on the eagle-like HIT-Hawk platform (1.80 m wingspan, 770 g, 100% payload ratio, ~14 m/s peak ground speed, 4× g peak linear acceleration during flapping). Synchronized data streams include onboard stereo RealSense D435i, redundant IMUs from a CUAV V5+ AHRS, RTK (1 cm horizontal/vertical) and indoor UWB (10 cm horizontal, 30 cm vertical) ground truth. A quantitative benchmark of four state-of-the-art VIO/multi-sensor pipelines — VINS-FUSION, GVINS, ORB-SLAM3, MSCKF-VIO — exposes severe robustness degradation under flapping-induced vibration and low-light multimodal flight: VINS-FUSION reaches the longest Mean Drift Occurrence Time (MDOT) of 47.73 s on static flapping, but all estimators show ATE RMSE of 5–25 m and >30% RPE translation error in flight, with sudden z-axis drift during the gliding-landing phase. The dataset thereby establishes a measurable gap between current VIO algorithms and FWAV mission-flight requirements such as waypoint navigation and obstacle avoidance in GPS-denied environments.

Introduction (click for detail)
The introduction establishes FWAVs as an emerging biologically-inspired aerial platform whose stealth, multi-modal flight, and high payload make them attractive for GPS-denied long-distance reconnaissance and confined-space obstacle avoidance, but whose self-localization capability lags multirotors badly. Three FWAV-specific obstacles (sensor-measurement perturbation, payload/power/trimming constraints, VIO drift in high-speed dynamic flight) are introduced as the rationale for a dedicated benchmark dataset, and the three claimed contributions (HIT-Hawk biomimetic platform, FWAF-VID dataset, SOTA VIO benchmark) are stated.
Related Works (click for detail)
Section II surveys MAV visual-inertial datasets along two axes (motion estimation challenge and ground-truth fidelity), then positions FWAF-VID against them. EuRoC pioneered indoor airborne benchmarks; Zurich Urban added long-endurance outdoor; UZH-FPV and SplitS Track introduced drone racing aggressiveness up to 100 km/h; Blackbird and VID-Multirotor added dynamics measurements; KAIST-VIO compared onboard computer platforms. The closest prior is GRIFFIN (Rodriguez-Gomez 2021), the only FWAV onboard perception dataset, but is critiqued for missing long-duration aggressive flight, FWAV-specific dynamics, and a SOTA VIO benchmark.
Method (click for detail)
Section III details HIT-Hawk's mechanical design (carbon-fiber fuselage with symmetric four-bar linkage, brushless DC motor with two-stage gear reducer, single-section reinforced-rib wing with spanwise deformation, integrated T-tail with 17.5° positive dihedral, CoG 16 cm from wing leading edge with aerodynamic center 4 cm aft for stability), the avionics (NTP-synchronized sensor suite, dual LiPo, Intel NUC11 onboard computer), and the frame-transformation chain among pixel/camera/IMU/body/world frames including extrinsics for the two camera mounting positions (45° downward and forward). The flapping motion is characterized via outdoor data: ground speed up to 14 m/s, ±55° pitch and ±95° roll within one second, minimum turning radius <2 m, and peak linear acceleration up to 4× g.
Experiment (click for detail)
Section V presents the static-flapping benchmark and the in-flight benchmark. The static-flapping experiment, evaluated via the new Mean Drift Occurrence Time (MDOT) metric, ranks VINS-FUSION best (47.73 s before z-axis drift exceeds 0.4 m), followed by ORB-SLAM3 (41.20 s), VINS-MONO (39.20 s) and MSCKF-VIO (15.95 s, drift onset at 1.54 Hz). The flight benchmark across four scenarios (slow/fast oval daylight, 8-character daylight, low-light outdoor, indoor oval bright) shows ORB-SLAM3 best in well-illuminated outdoor data, VINS-FUSION best in nighttime, and overall ATE RMSE 5–25 m with RPE >30%. All estimators experience sudden drift along the z-axis during the gliding-landing phase.
Discussion (click for detail)
The discussion (woven into Section V-B and the Conclusion) interprets the benchmark numbers as evidence that generic VIO algorithms hit a robustness wall on FWAV aggressive flight, with three concurrent stressors (high-frequency vibration, low light, multimodal flap-glide). RTK provides the cm-level ground truth that laser total stations cannot, while UWB at decimeter scale is the indoor compromise. The drift-suppression path forward requires integrating FWAV-specific motion models into VIO algorithms, not just collecting more data.
Conclusion (click for detail)
Section VI restates the three contributions (HIT-Hawk biomimetic platform, FWAF-VID multi-sensor synchronized aggressive-flight benchmark, SOTA VIO comparison exposing degradation) and explicitly identifies the unresolved problem (generic VIO frameworks degrade on FWAV aggressive flight) as the trigger for the future-work direction (incorporating FWAV dynamics into the VIO algorithm itself).
