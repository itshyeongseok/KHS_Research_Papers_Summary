Introduction — 연구 동기 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Ornithopters are gaining R&D attention for their agility, energy-efficient flapping–gliding modes and reduced collision risk versus multirotors, but their flapping vibrations and strict payload budget have kept perception and control offboard. This letter targets the harder unsolved sub-problem — onboard avoidance of unexpected dynamic obstacles — by exploiting the µs resolution, ~120 dB HDR and motion-blur robustness of event cameras with event-by-event processing on a single low-cost computer. The contribution is the first event-based dynamic obstacle avoidance designed for and validated on a flapping-wing robot, closing the loop at 250 Hz on the GRVC E-Flap.
Related Works — 관련 연구 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Reactive obstacle avoidance splits into map-based and map-less branches; this work follows the map-less line. Prior ornithopter perception (refs [15]–[17]) used monocular or stereo cameras but kept processing offboard or restricted scenarios. Existing event-based dynamic avoidance methods for quadrotors (refs [8]–[10]) require dual event cameras, powerful embedded computers and hovering or ≤1.5 m/s flight, so they cannot be directly mounted on an ornithopter — Table I summarises the comparison and motivates the present design.
Method — 새로운 접근 방법 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The pipeline has two modules. (1) Dynamic Object Motion Estimation chains a Time Filter on the Surface of Active Events, the *eFast event corner detector, an adapted ABMOF optical-flow stage and an event-by-event clusterer that maintains running mean flow per cluster. (2) Collision Risk Evaluation + Tail Control approximates the robot as a 2W×2H safety volume and the obstacle as a sphere of radius R'=R+b, computes admissible angles ψ*, θ* from a depth-dependent geometry and commands tail deflections u = u_react + Δu_model that combine a reactive law with a flapping-wing linearised-model correction. ASAP packaging keeps the 250 Hz loop overflow-free.
Experiment — 실험 방법과 결과 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Validation uses the GRVC E-Flap (95 cm length, 1.5 m wingspan, 510 g, 520 g maximum payload) with a Khadas VIM3 and a weight-trimmed DAVIS346 (FoV 68°×53.5°). Three obstacles — Small Box, Stuffed Toy, FitBall — are launched at distances of 0.5–6 m. Detection: 91.7% accuracy, 95.1% precision, 88.5% TPR, 4.2% FPR, with 11.2° RMSE on motion direction. Avoidance: 90.7% indoors, 92.0% outdoors regular illumination and 85.3% outdoors dark — averaging the headline 89.7%. Multi-obstacle scenarios drop to 74.2% due to cluster merging.
Discussion — 결과의 해석 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Event cameras are validated as superior to frame-based cameras under flapping vibration, and event-by-event processing is identified as the latency-critical design choice. A single camera plus a single low-cost computer is enough to fit the ornithopter payload while reaching quadrotor-grade detection. The remaining limits cluster around tail-control underactuation, multi-obstacle cluster merging, dark illumination, the τ trade-off, conservative safety inflation and the absence of static-obstacle support — each one is a candidate gap the user's research can deliberately attack.
Conclusion — 최종 요약 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The letter delivers the first event-based obstacle-avoidance system for large-scale flapping-wing robots, validated indoors and outdoors with an average avoidance success of 89.7% on a single-camera, single-computer onboard stack closing the loop at 250 Hz. Future work explicitly targets generalisation to other agile robots and integration with map-based static-obstacle avoidance for a complete sense-and-avoid stack — the precise direction in which the user's waypoint-mission research can claim its own contribution.
