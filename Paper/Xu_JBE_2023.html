Autonomous Formation Flight Control of Large-Sized Flapping-Wing Flying Robots Based on Leader–Follower Strategy
Tier 0 — 핵심
Hui Xu, Yuanpeng Wang, Erzhen Pan, Wenfu Xu, Dong Xue
Journal of Bionic Engineering, Vol. 20, 2023, pp. 2542–2558 · DOI: 10.1007/s42235-023-00402-5
🔍 핵심 인사이트 보기
논문 요약
The paper proposes the first autonomous leader–follower formation flight controller for Large-sized Flapping-Wing Flying Robots (LFWFRs), validated outdoors on two HIT-Phoenix robots (2.3 m wingspan, 675 g, 3–5 Hz flapping). A kinematics model captures the geometry of formations via a description matrix $C$, and consensus laws drive yaw, velocity and altitude of every follower toward the leader plus a desired relative distance and phase. The key novelty is a flapping-period-aware navigation method that averages flight state over three flapping cycles via a Hall sensor on the transmission, suppressing the 2–5° pitch fluctuations that otherwise cause overshoot and stall. Three formation modes — straight, circular and switching — are realised in simulation; the outdoor flight verifies the method despite GPS noise, wireless dropouts and tail roll/pitch coupling. Concrete numbers, hardware (STM32F405 + AS14B-TTL + DMA + FreeRTOS) and the protocol frame layout are published, making it a directly adaptable recipe for the user's eventual multi-FWAV missions.

Introduction — 연구 동기 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Birds use formation flight to save energy and extend range; the authors apply this idea to Large-sized Flapping-Wing Flying Robots (LFWFRs). The motivation is that single LFWFRs cannot meet the range/time/reliability bar required for outdoor missions, and the wing-weight + aerodynamic-centre coupling makes existing fixed/rotor-wing formation algorithms unsuitable. The contribution is the first autonomous leader–follower formation flight controller for LFWFRs, verified outdoors on the HIT-Phoenix platform.
Related Works — 관련 연구 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Existing FWFR research falls into prototype design (Robobee, Delfly, Robo Raven, Dove, FMAV, USTBird, HIT-Phoenix) and formation analysis. Prototype work mostly stops short of autonomous formation; formation work for FWFRs stays in indoor demonstrations or theory/simulation. Traditional formation algorithms — leader–follower, virtual structure, artificial potential field, behavior-based — were developed for fixed-wing/rotor-wing platforms whose trajectory tracking is much more accurate than LFWFRs can deliver, motivating a new periodic-flight-aware approach.
Method — 새로운 접근 방법 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The kinematics model uses geodetic and body-fixed coordinate systems with yaw–pitch–roll Euler sequence; aerodynamic forces $F_L, F_T$ and gravity $G$ define the per-LFWFR dynamics. A formation description matrix $C$ encodes desired relative distance $\rho$ and phase angle $\gamma$ for each follower; the relative geometry between LFWFRs $i,j$ produces $\dot\rho_{ij}, \dot\gamma_{ij}, \dot\psi_{ij}$. Yaw, velocity and altitude consistency laws drive each follower to track the leader plus an extra spacing term that depends on $\rho^d_{ij}$ and $\gamma^d_{ij}$. Hardware: STM32F405RGT6 + Cortex-M4 + FPU + AS14B-TTL wireless + DMA + FreeRTOS + custom 31-byte communication frame (SOH/data/EOT/check). The flapping-period-aware navigation method uses a Hall sensor on the transmission to detect rising edges, averaging flight state over three flapping cycles to suppress 2–5° pitch fluctuation.
Experiment — 실험 방법과 결과 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
Simulations validate three formation modes — linear (Table 3, $t=20$ s), circular (Table 4, $t=20$ s) and switching (Table 5, $t=40$ s, leader pivots from $\pi/4$ to $5\pi/4$ then formation switches to diagonal). Followers track leader yaw, velocity and altitude within ~5 s with errors fluctuating within ±1 m. Outdoor experiment uses two HIT-Phoenix LFWFRs at 10 m apart and $3\pi/4$ phase, after take-off above safe altitude. The flapping-period-aware navigation reduces pitch fluctuation visibly versus 50 Hz raw sampling; the formation algorithm preliminarily realises outdoor formation despite GPS errors, wireless dropouts and tail roll/pitch coupling.
Discussion — 결과의 해석 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
The paper closes the formation-flight gap for LFWFRs experimentally, but several practical bottlenecks remain visible — GPS precision, wireless reliability, flapping-frequency–velocity nonlinearity, tail roll/pitch coupling, and immature take-off/landing. The flapping-period-aware navigation method is the most transferable single contribution: a simple Hall-sensor + 3-cycle averaging trick that any FWAV using flapping wings could adopt. The communication-protocol design and STM32F405 + AS14B-TTL hardware recipe are publication-grade reference designs. Altitude is the next frontier.
Conclusion — 최종 요약 클릭하여 자세히 ▸
A leader–follower formation flight controller for LFWFRs is proposed, validated in simulation across straight/circular/switching modes and verified outdoors on two HIT-Phoenix robots. The flapping-period-aware navigation absorbs fuselage fluctuation. Future work focuses on improving 3-D trajectory keeping accuracy and scaling to ultra-multiple LFWFRs.
